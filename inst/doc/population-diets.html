Diet scoring in Life’s Essential 8

Diet is one of the eight components of the American Heart Association’s (AHA) Life’s Essential 8 (LE8). essential8 supports three diet measures: MEPA, DASH, and HEI-2015. Each describes diet quality in a different way. This package first calculates or accepts a diet score, then applies the LE8 scoring rules.

This article explains where the measures came from, how they become LE8 points, and which inputs to use. Examples apply to adults aged 20 or older; for children, see vignette("pediatric-scoring"). The packaged NHANES references describe adults and are refused for pediatric rows.

Where the diet measures came from

MEPA: a short dietary screener

The Mediterranean Eating Pattern for Americans (MEPA) tool asks about foods associated with a Mediterranean-style eating pattern. It was evaluated by Cerwinske and colleagues in 2017. The AHA adapted it for rapid individual assessment in its 2022 LE8 advisory.

essential8 implements the advisory’s modified 16-item version. Each item earns zero or one point, giving a total from 0 to 16. Fixed bands convert that total to LE8 points. No population comparison is needed. The Get Started vignette covers the MEPA inputs.

DASH: an eating pattern and a research score

DASH stands for Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. The eating pattern was tested in an NHLBI-supported feeding trial to study its effects on blood pressure. It emphasizes fruits, vegetables, and low-fat dairy, with less saturated and total fat.

Researchers later developed scores to measure how closely a person’s diet follows that pattern. essential8 uses an adaptation of the eight-component score described by Fung and colleagues in 2008. Its total ranges from 8 to 40. Other DASH scores exist, so the name “DASH” alone does not establish that two scores use the same definition.

HEI-2015: alignment with dietary guidelines

The Healthy Eating Index (HEI) measures how closely a diet follows the Dietary Guidelines for Americans. USDA released the first HEI in 1995; later versions were developed with the National Cancer Institute (NCI). HEI-2015 reflects the 2015-2020 guidelines and has 13 components that sum to a score from 0 to 100. See the NCI history and standards.

HEI-2015 rewards foods and nutrients to include, such as whole grains and vegetables, and limits others, such as sodium and added sugars. Most components use intake per 1,000 kcal, so eating more food does not by itself produce a higher score. The package uses HEI-2015 because that is the version specified in the 2022 LE8 definition.

How diet scores become LE8 points

The AHA advisory uses modified MEPA for individual assessment and DASH or HEI-2015 for population assessment. These are alternative ways to score the same LE8 diet component. They do not add extra components to LE8.

Diet measure Original score How it becomes an LE8 diet score
Modified MEPA 0-16 Apply fixed MEPA bands
Fung-style DASH 8-40 Compare the total with a reference population
HEI-2015 0-100 Compare the total with a reference population

A raw diet score, also called an index, is the total before conversion to LE8 points.

For DASH and HEI-2015, a percentile describes a score’s position in the reference population. For example, the 75th percentile marks the upper quarter of that distribution. The advisory assigns LE8 points as follows:

MEPA total DASH or HEI-2015 percentile LE8 diet points
0-3 Below 25 0
4-7 25 to below 50 25
8-11 50 to below 75 50
12-14 75 to below 95 80
15-16 95 or higher 100

These bands come from the AHA LE8 advisory, Table 1. A raw HEI-2015 score of 70 is therefore different from the 70th percentile or an LE8 diet score of 70. The reference determines which LE8 band contains that raw score.

The resulting diet_score contributes to the LE8 composite in the same way as the other seven components. score_le8() averages available component scores when at least min_components are present; the default is seven.

What the packaged reference represents

The advisory initially recommends NHANES 2015-2018 as the reference for population diet scoring. NHANES is the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey. essential8 includes references derived from those survey years and the matching USDA Food Patterns Equivalents Database (FPED), which converts foods into common food-group units.

The NHANES references describe 8,241 adults aged 20 or older, excluding known pregnancy, with two reliable dietary recalls and complete required inputs. The builder averages the same two days’ foods, nutrients, and energy before scoring. It uses the combined dietary weight (WTDR2D / 2), survey strata, and sampling units to calculate weighted cutpoints: the score values that mark the percentile boundaries.

The DASH reference uses an FPED adaptation specific to essential8: essential8_fung_fped_v1. It uses pooled intake quintiles across sexes and the food definitions listed below. An existing DASH score must match these definitions to use this reference. HEI-2015 inputs should also match the reference’s method of combining recalls.

Choose the inputs you have

Use one diet method and one type of diet input per call:

Available data What to supply
MEPA responses The 16 screener items with diet_method = "mepa"
DASH or HEI-2015 food and nutrient summaries The raw input columns described below
An existing DASH or HEI-2015 score A column named dash or hei2015

The examples use dplyr and the native pipe (|>, available in R 4.1+). We start with two packaged example adults so the other LE8 components are available.

library(essential8)
library(dplyr)

example_file <- system.file(
  "extdata", "le8-adult-example.csv", package = "essential8"
)

adults <- read.csv(example_file) |>
  as_tibble() |>
  slice_head(n = 2) |>
  select(-starts_with("expected_")) |>
  mutate(id = c("adult_1", "adult_2"))

An existing diet score

Add an HEI-2015 score and name the reference explicitly:

adults |>
  mutate(hei2015 = c(45, 70)) |>
  score_le8(diet_method = "hei2015", reference = "nhanes_2015_2018") |>
  select(id, hei2015, diet_score)
#> # A tibble: 2 × 3
#>   id      hei2015 diet_score
#>   <chr>     <dbl>      <dbl>
#> 1 adult_1      45         25
#> 2 adult_2      70         80

The raw scores of 45 and 70 receive 25 and 80 LE8 points with this reference. For an existing DASH score, use a dash column and diet_method = "dash". The original score stays in its input column; dash_total and hei2015_total are filled only when calculated from raw inputs.

An existing percentile

A percentile already contains the population comparison, so no reference lookup is needed:

adults |>
  mutate(diet_value = c(35, 98)) |>
  score_le8(diet_method = "dash") |>
  select(id, diet_value, diet_score)
#> # A tibble: 2 × 3
#>   id      diet_value diet_score
#>   <chr>        <dbl>      <dbl>
#> 1 adult_1         35         25
#> 2 adult_2         98        100

These percentiles receive 25 and 100 LE8 points. Supply 95 for the 95th percentile, rather than 0.95. Fractional proportions are rejected. The generic diet_method = "percentile" option uses the same LE8 bands.

Calculate DASH from raw inputs

The Fung-style score compares each of eight intakes with reference quintiles: five intake groups, each containing about one fifth of the reference population. Fruit, vegetables, nuts and legumes, whole grains, and low-fat dairy earn 1-5 points as intake increases. Sodium, red and processed meat, and sweetened beverages earn 5-1 points. The eight scores sum to dash_total.

There are two population comparisons here: intake quintiles create the DASH total, then total-score cutpoints assign the LE8 band. Both come from the packaged reference by default.

Prepare the daily inputs

For the packaged reference, use these FPED-based definitions. Cup and ounce equivalents are standardized food-group amounts, not necessarily the volume or weight of the food as eaten.

Column Daily input
dash_fruits Total fruit cup equivalents, including juice
dash_vegetables Dark-green cup equivalents plus twice the sum of red/orange, other, and other-starchy vegetable cup equivalents; exclude white potatoes and legumes
dash_nuts_and_legumes Protein ounce equivalents from legumes, nuts/seeds, and soy
dash_whole_grains Whole-grain ounce equivalents
dash_low_fat_dairy Skim-milk cup equivalents plus four times low-fat cheese cup equivalents, plus yogurt cup equivalents, using the reference’s food-code lists
dash_sodium Sodium in mg/day
dash_red_and_processed_meat Cured-meat ounce equivalents divided by 1.5 plus meat/organ ounce equivalents divided by 4
dash_sweetened_beverages Added-sugar teaspoon equivalents from the reference’s beverage codes, multiplied by 4/26
dash_energy_kcal Positive kcal/day, including alcohol

Sodium is standardized to 2,000 kcal using sodium / energy_kcal * 2000. Supply unadjusted mg/day; do not apply this adjustment twice. Average the same recalls’ daily inputs before scoring.

Score the inputs

dash_inputs <- tibble(
  dash_fruits = c(1, 2),
  dash_vegetables = c(2, 3),
  dash_nuts_and_legumes = c(0.5, 1),
  dash_whole_grains = c(1, 2),
  dash_low_fat_dairy = c(1, 2),
  dash_sodium = c(3000, 2000),
  dash_red_and_processed_meat = c(1, 0.5),
  dash_sweetened_beverages = c(1, 0),
  dash_energy_kcal = c(2000, 2000)
)

adults |>
  bind_cols(dash_inputs) |>
  score_le8(diet_method = "dash", reference = "nhanes_2015_2018") |>
  select(id, dash_total, diet_score)
#> # A tibble: 2 × 3
#>   id      dash_total diet_score
#>   <chr>        <int>      <dbl>
#> 1 adult_1         27         80
#> 2 adult_2         36        100

Raw DASH scoring also returns the eight component scores, such as dash_low_fat_dairy_score, so you can see which quintile each intake fell into.

Calculate HEI-2015 from raw inputs

HEI-2015 uses fixed nutrition standards to calculate its raw score. Its nine adequacy components cover fruit, vegetables, whole grains, dairy, protein foods, and the balance of unsaturated to saturated fats. Its four moderation components cover refined grains, sodium, added sugars, and saturated fats. Each component contributes up to 5 or 10 points, for a maximum total of 100. See the NCI scoring standards.

Scores between the minimum and maximum standards are proportional. For example, 0.8 cup equivalents of total fruit per 1,000 kcal earns all 5 fruit points; 0.4 earns 2.5. Higher intake beyond a component’s maximum standard does not add points. After summing the 13 components, the total is compared with the reference to assign LE8 points.

Prepare the daily inputs

Columns, all prefixed with hei2015_ Units
energy_kcal Positive kcal/day, including alcohol
total_fruits, whole_fruits, total_vegetables, greens_and_beans, dairy Cup equivalents/day
whole_grains, total_protein_foods, seafood_and_plant_proteins, refined_grains Ounce equivalents/day
polyunsaturated_fat, monounsaturated_fat, saturated_fat Grams/day
sodium mg/day
added_sugars Teaspoon equivalents/day

Include juice in total fruit but exclude it from whole fruit. Count legumes in both vegetable and protein groups, using the appropriate equivalents. Average the same recalls’ inputs before scoring. essential8 calculates the densities, fat ratio, and energy percentages from these amounts, following the NCI algorithm.

Use a different reference when needed

For existing scores or raw HEI-2015 inputs, diet_reference() defines total-score cutpoints at the 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th percentiles:

custom <- diet_reference(
  p25 = 40, p50 = 55, p75 = 65, p95 = 80,
  method = "hei2015", label = "Example reference"
)

adults |>
  mutate(hei2015 = c(45, 70)) |>
  score_le8(diet_method = "hei2015", reference = custom) |>
  select(id, hei2015, diet_score)
#> # A tibble: 2 × 3
#>   id      hei2015 diet_score
#>   <chr>     <dbl>      <dbl>
#> 1 adult_1      45         25
#> 2 adult_2      70         80

A reference must match the diet method, score definition, and population. For raw DASH with a different definition, supply dash_cutpoints: a list with four intake-quintile cutpoints for each of the eight fields and four total-score cutpoints in total. A reference containing only total-score cutpoints cannot define the intake quintiles.

Explicit dash_cutpoints or hei2015_cutpoints bypass reference. With explicit DASH cutpoints, input units must match those used to derive the cutpoints. Energy is not required and sodium is not standardized. For raw HEI-2015, hei2015_cutpoints is a vector of four total-score cutpoints.

Tied cutpoints are allowed. Equality with an intake-quintile cutpoint stays in the lower quintile; equality with a total-score cutpoint enters the higher LE8 band. The four total cutpoints identify bands, not an exact percentile for every possible score.

Practical details

  • Choose one method per call. Do not combine raw inputs with an existing index or observed percentile. An entirely missing diet_value column is ignored.
  • For custom column names, use mappings such as dash_columns = c(fruits = "fruit_intake", energy_kcal = "calories"). hei2015_columns works the same way. Names are matched without regard to case.
  • Every raw input column must be present once raw scoring is selected; an absent column is an error, so a misspelled name cannot silently produce missing diet scores. Missing values within a column are allowed.
  • Missing inputs stay missing. A raw total requires all its components; min_components controls the LE8 composite, not a partial diet total. Available HEI component scores are still returned.

See ?score_le8 for the full input rules. The repository’s reference documentation records the FPED conversions, food-code lists, sample selection, and survey methods. Its build script and source manifest record how the packaged references were produced.
